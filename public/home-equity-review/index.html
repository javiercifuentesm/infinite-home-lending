Understand before you decide

Your equity.
A clearer picture.

A closed-end second mortgage—commonly called a home equity loan—lets you borrow a lump sum against available equity. It usually has a fixed rate and scheduled payments. Equity is your home’s current value minus all outstanding loans secured by it. Not all of that equity is necessarily available to borrow.

You retain your first mortgage and add a separate loan. The right question is whether that new structure supports your goals after accounting for costs and risk.

Good questions, clear answers

Before you borrow.

Will this change my existing mortgage rate?

A separate home equity loan does not replace your first mortgage or change its contractual terms. If your first mortgage has an adjustable rate, that rate can still change as provided in the existing agreement. You continue paying the first mortgage and make payments on the new loan.

How much equity can I access?

Available borrowing depends on the property’s value, existing liens, lender limits and your qualifications. Your total equity is not the same as the amount you can borrow.

Will debt consolidation save me money?

Possibly, but it is not guaranteed. Compare the rates, fees, remaining terms and total repayment—not just the monthly payment. A longer term can reduce the payment while increasing total interest.

What are the costs and risks?

Costs can include lender, valuation and closing fees, depending on the program. Your home secures the debt; failure to repay could result in foreclosure. Avoid rebuilding balances on accounts paid off through consolidation.

Does requesting a conversation affect my credit?

No credit check takes place on this page. Requesting a conversation does not authorize a credit inquiry. A later application and any credit authorization would be handled separately.
